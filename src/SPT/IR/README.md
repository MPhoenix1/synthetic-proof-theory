# Induction–recursion

Dybjer–Setzer induction–recursion on SPT's base, in two presentations. Without induction–recursion: Dybjer–Setzer descriptions and their algebras; the generated part of an algebra as the closed trees of a signature, with Dybjer–Setzer's induction and its initiality; morphisms of algebras and translations of descriptions; the smallness principle as a statement, with its lowerings; the polynomial codes PN of Ghani, McBride, Nordvall Forsberg and Spahn for indexed induction–recursion, with Dybjer–Setzer's general indexed codes; and the rules of Shulman's univalent universes, with least generations. With Agda's own induction–recursion: one type for all descriptions ([Universal](Universal/README.md)) and one family for all schemes of indexed polynomial induction–recursion ([Indexed](Indexed/README.md)).

[Universal](Universal.agda) and [Indexed](Indexed.agda) are the only modules here that define induction–recursive types, and within IR only the modules under `Universal/` and `Indexed/` build on them. The folder depends only on SPT's Base and Foundation; no module imports Mahlo or the standard library. The index [IR](../IR.agda) imports every module. What the Mahlo rule gives for these descriptions, including the equivalence of the smallness principle with the Mahlo rule for the ambient family, is in [Mahlo/DS](../Mahlo/DS/README.md).

## Descriptions, shapes and layers

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Description](Description.agda) | Dybjer–Setzer descriptions with arities of level s (`DS D E`, and `Desc D = DS D D`): ι gives an output, σ a small sum, and δ an A-indexed family of recursive inputs whose decodings select the continuation. An indexed family is a carrier of any level κ with a decoding (`Indexed`); a description acts on it by its shapes and their outputs (`Shape`, `Mark`), at the level s ⊔ κ. |
| [Algebra](Algebra.agda) | An algebra of a description γ (`Algebra κ γ`, a record): a carrier `K` of level κ, a decoding `value`, and an operation on the shapes over the carrier. Its law, kept separate, says that the operation decodes to the output of each shape (`Law`). The carrier level is a parameter: an algebra is small when κ is the level of the arities. The algebra on the outputs themselves, at their own level, has its law by refl (`identity`, `identity-β`); its generated part is the raised fixed point of γ (Comparison). |
| [Action](Action.agda) | Maps into the fibers of one decoding over another act on shapes, keeping their outputs. A δ continuation read at two identified assignments has the same shapes, with the same outputs (`shift`). A shape over Z with a point of W over the decoding of each of its inputs gives a shape over W with the same output, under extensionality for functions from an arity into the decodings (`assemble`). Comparison's `assemble`, Smallness/Lowering's `carry` and Morphism's `step` are this action; Polynomial/Action is its form for PN codes. |
| [Restriction](Restriction.agda) | Recursive inputs of shapes: the positions of a shape are the arities of its δ nodes, with the point chosen at each (`Position`, `input`). Shapes over the restriction of an indexed family (K , value) to a family Q over K, the pairs (k , q) with q : Q k decoded through K: an argument over the restriction has heads over (K , value) with the same output (`heads`, `heads-β`) and a point of Q at each input of its heads (`children`), and these are its inputs (`transfer`, `transfer-β`). It splits into a layer, an argument over (K , value) with a point of Q at each input (`Layer`, `split`), and layers join back (`join`). Joining a split gives the argument back, and the heads and inputs of a join are the layer's, by induction on the description with no extensionality (`join-split`, `heads-join`, `position-join`, `input-join`); splitting a join gives the layer back under extensionality for families over positions (`split-join`, through `points`, `children-points`, `points-η`). Generated is this at the derivations, and [Mahlo/DS/Univalent/Construction](../Mahlo/DS/Univalent/Construction.agda) at a generation. |
| [Layers](Layers.agda) | A shape over a family as a layer over the outputs. The skeleton of a shape reads its inputs as outputs, with the same output (`skeleton`, `read`), and hypotheses at its inputs collect at the skeleton's positions, naturally in the hypotheses (`collect`, `collect-natural`). Under extensionality for the arities, shapes over W and layers with points of the fibers of W's decoding at their inputs, with their outputs kept, are one datum (`Marked`, `Layered`): `up` is Action's assembly from the identity family, at extensionality adjusted to send the constant homotopy to refl (`normal`), and `down` is the skeleton with its canonical points (`canonical`). Action's assembly from any family is `up` of the skeleton with the collected points (`assembled`), and `up` and `down` are inverse (`up-down`, and `down-up` through the induction `layers`). |

## Generated parts

| [Induction/Generated](Induction/Generated.agda) | The generated part satisfies induction on source arguments, Dybjer and Setzer's elimination rule: the specification of [Base/Induction](../Base/Induction.agda) at Generated's introduction, whose arguments are the source arguments with their inputs. | [Identifications](Identifications.agda) | Identifications of shapes and of generated elements, layer by layer. An identification of generated elements gives an agreement of their source arguments with no hypothesis (`identified-elements-agree`). Under extensionality for families over positions, unrolling is an isomorphism (`unrolling`), and identifications of generated elements are exactly agreements of their source arguments (`generated-identifications`). Equal decodings do not give equal elements. |
| [Identifications/Discriminator](Identifications/Discriminator.agda) | At δ, two shapes whose child maps decode alike do not agree, since agreement identifies the maps themselves (`children-decode-alike`, `children-differ`, `not-identified`). The isomorphisms compute: refl goes to the trivial agreement and back (`forward-refl`, `backward-trivial`, `agree-refl`). |
| [Predicate](Predicate.agda) | Predicates that a description preserves: Q on the decodings of the recursive inputs and R on the outputs follow the description's constructors and the actual returns of its δ nodes (`Good`, an inductive family: `terminal`, `parameter`, `recursive`). A shape whose inputs decode into Q has an output in R (`preserve`). | [Comparison](Comparison.agda) | The raised fixed point μγ is the generated part of the identity algebra: an ordinary inductive family at the outputs' level, decoded by its index. Each derivation of an algebra M with its law maps into μγ at its own decoding, the decodings kept by refl, with no extensionality (`layer`, `output`, `roll`, `to`, `decode`). | [Comparison/Discriminator](Comparison/Discriminator.agda) | μγ decodes by refl at a closed description: for the description whose leaf is 𝟚 and whose step sends a decoded input X to X → X, the first codes decode to 𝟚, 𝟚 → 𝟚 and (𝟚 → 𝟚) → (𝟚 → 𝟚) (`decode-leaf`, `decode-next`, `decode-first`, `decode-second`). And `to` keeps recursive depth: for a leaf and a unary step whose outputs are all the unit type, the one-point algebra satisfies the law, so its carrier forgets all depth, and still the image of the second step over the leaf has depth two in μγ (`depth`, `two`). |

## The smallness principle

The smallness principle for a level ℓ (`Smallness ℓ`, a record type): every description with arities of level ℓ and outputs in `Set ℓ` has an algebra whose carrier lies in `Set ℓ`, with its law.

```text
Smallness ℓ  =  Π (γ : Desc (Set ℓ)). Σ (K : Set ℓ) (value : K → Set ℓ)
                  (operation : Shape γ (K , value) → K).
                  Π z. value (operation z) ＝ Mark γ (K , value) z
```

It asks for an algebra on a small carrier with its decoding law, and nothing else: no induction and no leastness; descriptions with outputs outside `Set ℓ` are not covered. Without smallness there is always such an algebra one level up, on the outputs themselves (`Algebra.identity`), and its generated part is the raised fixed point μγ. IR states the principle and does not inhabit it. [Mahlo/DS/Ambient](../Mahlo/DS/Ambient.agda) proves it interderivable with the Mahlo rule for the ambient family: the rule gives it with no hypothesis, and it gives the rule under extensionality for families of types.

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Smallness](Smallness.agda) | The principle (`Smallness`, with fields `algebra` and `law`) and every rule of induction–recursion from it, through the generated part of the small algebra, an ordinary inductive family: formation and decoding (`G`, `t`), introduction with its decoding equation (`make`, `make-β`), and induction into every level with its computation equation by refl (`Method`, `ind`, `ind-β`). |
| [Smallness/Lowering](Smallness/Lowering.agda) | The principle as lowerings of the raised fixed points. A lowering of γ is a small type with maps both ways to μγ that keep the decodings (`Lowering`, a record): a weak presentation of μγ by a small type, the two maps not asserted inverse. Under extensionality for index families, a small algebra gives a lowering through its generated part (`lowered`, by Comparison), and a lowering gives a small algebra: a small shape is carried to μγ by `up`, rolled there, and brought down (`carry`, `raise`, `raise-law`). So, under that extensionality, the principle holds exactly when every description has a lowering (`lowerings`, `smallness`). |
| [Smallness/Initiality](Smallness/Initiality.agda) | The induction–recursion that smallness derives has a unique eliminator: every valuation satisfying the computation equations of congruent methods is Smallness's induction (`ind-unique`). This is Generated/Initiality at a description's small algebra, which also gives there the relational form, the fundamental theorem and fusion. At the ambient Mahlo rule it is [Mahlo/DS/Ambient/Initiality](../Mahlo/DS/Ambient/Initiality.agda)'s `mahlo-ind-unique`. Initiality among lawful algebras is Morphism/Initiality's `small-unique` and `small-initial`. |
| [Smallness/Discriminator](Smallness/Discriminator.agda) | The law of a small algebra has content. For the description with two outputs, unit and empty, the one-point algebra decoding to unit fails the law (`one-point-fails`), and every algebra keeps its two operations apart (`distinct`): the carrier must separate what the outputs separate. So do the decoding laws of a lowering: its `down` keeps the two raised codes apart (`separates`). |

## Morphisms of algebras

A morphism sends each code to a code of the target together with an identification of their decodings. The morphisms out of an algebra are the sections of a step, so [Foundation/Induction/Initiality](../Foundation/Induction/Initiality.agda) gives their initiality for every algebra with induction on source arguments, and the generated parts are such algebras (Induction/Generated).

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Morphism](Morphism.agda) | Morphisms of algebras of a description, with their laws. The fibers of N's decoding over M's decodings (`Fibers`) have a step: N's operation at the shape Action assembles from a shape of M with points of the fibers at its inputs, its decoding read back along M's law (`step`). A morphism is a section of that step (`Morphism`): each code goes to a code of N with an identification of decodings, and the image of an operation is N's operation, with its decoding. Both are taken at extensionality for functions from the arities into the outputs, which the assembly uses. |
| [Morphism/Initiality](Morphism/Initiality.agda) | Initiality among algebras with their laws. For an algebra with induction on source arguments and its computation identity, the fold into every lawful algebra is a morphism (`fold`); under extensionality over the positions every morphism is the fold pointwise (`unique`), and under the extensionality of Foundation/Induction/Initiality the morphisms form a contractible type (`initial`). The generated part of every algebra is such an algebra (`generated`, by Induction/Generated): its fold is unique pointwise under extensionality for functions out of types at the arities' level only, into the outputs, the derivations and the fibers (`generated-fold`, `generated-unique`), and the morphism type is contractible when extensionality for functions on the generated part and its source arguments is added (`generated-initial`). So is the induction–recursion of smallness (`small-unique`, `small-initial`); at the ambient Mahlo rule this is [Mahlo/DS/Ambient/Initiality](../Mahlo/DS/Ambient/Initiality.agda). |
| [Morphism/Uniqueness](Morphism/Uniqueness.agda) | Uniqueness of the generated part. The generated parts of two lawful algebras of one description are isomorphic: the folds each way compose to a morphism from a generated part to itself, which the fold's uniqueness identifies with the identity (`generated-parts-are-isomorphic`), under extensionality for functions out of the arities. So every lawful algebra, whatever its carrier, has the raised fixed point as its generated part, up to isomorphism. |
| [Morphism/Composition](Morphism/Composition.agda) | Identity and composition of morphisms of algebras, at Layers' adjusted extensionality, as the morphisms along the identity translation (Translation/Morphism). The identity sends each code to itself with refl (`identity`); the composite carries each point of the middle algebra's fiber by the second morphism, extended to the fibers of its decoding (`compose`). On valuations the identity is a two-sided unit and composition is associative, pointwise (`identity-right` by computation; `identity-left` and `associative` by path induction on the identification of decodings, `fiber-unit`, `extend-associative`). |
| [Morphism/Fusion](Morphism/Fusion.agda) | Fusion among algebras with their laws: a morphism after the fold is the fold into its codomain, pointwise in the fibers, so with the identification of decodings (`fusion`), for any algebra with induction on source arguments and its computation identity. It is uniqueness of the fold, since a composite of morphisms is a morphism (Composition). The generated part of every algebra is such an algebra (`generated-fusion`). |
| [Morphism/Discriminator](Morphism/Discriminator.agda) | The decoding identifications of morphisms are load-bearing. For the description with one constant constructor at e (`constant`), every map from the generated part of the one-point algebra (`point`, `point-law`) into the one-point algebra preserves codes (`Codes`, `codes`). At the introduction the step is (⋆ , refl) by computation, so every morphism has decoding refl there (`untwisted`, `pinned`), and the map with a loop p there is a morphism only when p is refl (`straight`, `twisted`, `twisted-not-morphism`): if preservation of codes determined valuations, every loop at e would be refl (`codes-undetermined`). With a second output, the identity algebra has no morphism into the one-point algebra (`no-identity-morphism`). |

## Translations of descriptions

A translation reads each position of the target description back at a position of the source, along a map of outputs. Along the identity translation, morphisms along a translation are the morphisms of algebras, and under the extensionality of Morphism/Initiality the fold along a translation satisfies the functor laws.

A position may be erased or used twice. A translation acts on layers by its shape map, reading points back (`Pointed`, `translate`), and so on shapes over any family, through Layers (`act`). Along the identity translation these are Morphism's morphisms, at Layers' adjusted extensionality (`steps-agree`, `along-morphism`, `morphism-along`). |
| [Translation/Fold](Translation/Fold.agda) | Initiality along translations, for an algebra with induction on source arguments and its computation identity: along τ the fold into every lawful algebra of the target is a morphism along τ, every morphism along τ is the fold pointwise, and the morphisms form a contractible type (`fold`, `unique`, `initial`), by Foundation/Induction/Initiality at the step of Translation/Morphism. | [Translation/Initiality](Translation/Initiality.agda) | The action of translations on generated parts, as a fold, with its functor laws: Translation/Fold at the generated part of a lawful algebra with its source induction. Along τ the generated part has a morphism into every lawful algebra of the target (`fold`), every morphism along τ is the fold pointwise (`unique`), and the morphisms form a contractible type (`initial`); the fold along the identity translation is the identity (`fold-identity`), and the fold along a composite is the composite of the folds (`fold-compose`). |
| [Translation/Fusion](Translation/Fusion.agda) | Fusion along a translation. | [Translation/Discriminator](Translation/Discriminator.agda) | Translations of closed descriptions over numbers. ## Indexed polynomial induction–recursion

The codes are the polynomial codes PN of Ghani, McBride, Nordvall Forsberg and Spahn (*Variations on inductive-recursive definitions*, MFCS 2017, Def. 22), with indices: a recursive input at an index (`var`), a constant (`con`), a dependent sum whose continuation reads the information of its first part (`sigma`), and a dependent product (`pi`). The paper defines its codes together with their information by induction–recursion. Here the information is the index of a code, so the codes are an ordinary inductive family, one level up, and no induction–recursion is used. Dybjer–Setzer's codes embed in PN (the paper's Prop. 28, with `sigma (pi A (λ _ → var i)) F` for δ); the paper conjectures that the embedding is strict, and its model (Thm. 27) assumes a Mahlo cardinal and a 1-inaccessible cardinal above it. Dybjer–Setzer's codes for indexed induction–recursion (*Indexed induction–recursion*, JLAP 2006) are polynomial codes with an output. Small algebras of these rules, from smallness and from the ambient Mahlo rule, are in [Mahlo/DS](../Mahlo/DS/README.md).

So every element is an introduction (`unroll`, `unroll-make`, `make-unroll`), and induction pulls back along `split`, computing through the half-adjoint coherence (`Method`, `ind`, `ind-β`; `ind` is sealed, and its uses go through `ind-β`). Hypotheses at the argument's own positions give the elimination rule on literal arguments (`Literal`, `transfer`, `Step`, `source`, `source-β`). |
| [Polynomial/Induction/Initiality](Polynomial/Induction/Initiality.agda) | Pointwise initiality of induction on layers and on literal arguments, with no extensionality beyond the one introduction uses. Relatedness is pointwise, at the positions of a layer and of an argument (`Layer-Related`, `Literal-Related`, `literal-values`). The lax law at arguments holds at the image of each argument and is carried to every layer by the extension of the method (`Lax`, `Lawful`, `layer-law`), where the initiality of derivations applies (`ind-unique-rel`); `transfer` keeps relatedness, `carry` by path induction (`carry-related`, `transfer-related`), so the lax law at literal hypotheses gives relational uniqueness of induction on literal arguments (`Literal-Lax`, `source-unique-rel`). |
| [Polynomial/Factorization](Polynomial/Factorization.agda) | Arguments over a union of stages lie in one stage. The stages carry a preorder with joins and bounds of families indexed by small types (`Stages`, a record; the bound is a function, not a mere existence, as in [Mahlo/Heights/Regular](../Mahlo/Heights/Regular.agda)), and each element of the union carries its stage (`rank`). An argument lies in a stage when the inputs at its positions do (`Within`); a code is bounded at a stage when its paths over every later stage that stop at a product's arity request small types (`Over`, `Arity`, `Bounded`). Every argument of a bounded code then lies in one stage (`factor`), and so does every argument of the rules of a scheme that grows boundedly (`Growth`, `onto`): Lemma 4.3 of [Indexed induction–recursion in simplicial sets](../--drafts--/IndexedUniversalIR.md), with the argument and its information kept, so no continuation is moved; no transport and no extensionality are used. |
| [Polynomial/Factorization/Discriminator](Polynomial/Factorization/Discriminator.agda) | The factorization at the finite stages. The numbers are stages whose small types are the finitely covered ones, bounded by maxima (`numbers`). A product of two recursive inputs lies in the stage of its larger input (`pair-stage`, by refl). The product over the numbers of the numbers themselves lies in no stage (`unbounded`), so at the finite stages its growth hypothesis fails (`no-growth`): the bound at products is what the factorization uses. |
| [General](General.agda) | Dybjer–Setzer's rules for general indexed induction–recursion (JLAP 2006, §4.7, §4.8), once for every inductive family of a general code (`Inductive-Family`, a record): carriers with readouts, the arguments of the code's polynomial over them compared with its shapes clause by clause, introduction at located arguments with its readout law, and induction on them with the hypotheses it supplies. Shapes and located arguments are isomorphic (`Located`, `arguments`); introduction lands at the index its argument computes, with its decoding equation (`intro`, `decoding`); a method carried along the isomorphism gives the elimination rule (`Method`, `Fiber`, `prepared`, `method`, `ind`), whose computation equation holds wherever the family's induction has its own (`Computation`, `ind-β`). The universal family ([Indexed/General](Indexed/General.agda)) and the generated part of any algebra ([Polynomial/General](Polynomial/General.agda)) are inductive families. |
| [General/Initiality](General/Initiality.agda) | Pointwise initiality of the rule, once for every inductive family whose own induction has it (`Pointwise-Initiality`, a record: relatedness of hypotheses at a located argument, a valuation's hypotheses there, relational uniqueness of the family's induction, and relatedness read forward at the image of a shape). The lifting of a relation to the rule's hypotheses at every subcode (`Lifted`); relational uniqueness under the lax law, the law carried from the images of shapes to every located argument (`Lax`, `Lawful`, `lawful-at-image`, `carried-law`, `ind-unique-rel`); the fundamental theorem (`Relational`, `ind-rel`); uniqueness for congruent methods (`Identical`, `Congruent`, `Homomorphic`, `ind-unique`, `ind-homomorphic`); fusion (`mapped`, `Graph`, `graph-agreement`, `Carries`, `ind-fusion`); under extensionality every method is congruent (`agreement`, `congruent`). [Indexed/General/Initiality](Indexed/General/Initiality.agda) and [Polynomial/General/Initiality](Polynomial/General/Initiality.agda) give the record. |
| [Polynomial/General](Polynomial/General.agda) | Dybjer–Setzer's rules for general indexed induction–recursion (JLAP 2006, §4.7, §4.8) on the generated part of any algebra of a general code. Shapes are the arguments of the code's polynomial, clause by clause, over any family, with no extensionality (`to`, `from`, `from-to`, `to-from`, `output-to`), and hypotheses at the positions of an argument give the ordinary ones (`hyp-forward`, `hyp-forward-β`). Under extensionality for families of information over the arities, introduction and induction on literal arguments make the generated part an inductive family of the code (`generated-part`), its computation identity holding under extensionality for the motive (`generated-part-computation`), so the rules of [General](General.agda) hold there. Smallness and the ambient Mahlo rule give such algebras in [Mahlo/DS/Polynomial/General](../Mahlo/DS/Polynomial/General.agda). |
| [Polynomial/General/Initiality](Polynomial/General/Initiality.agda) | Pointwise initiality of the rule on generated parts. Relatedness at a located argument is relatedness at the positions of its argument, read forward to the lifting at a shape (`lifted-forward`), so the generated part has the record of [General/Initiality](General/Initiality.agda) (`generated-part-initiality`), from the initiality of induction on literal arguments (Polynomial/Induction/Initiality). |
| [Polynomial/General/Discriminator](Polynomial/General/Discriminator.agda) | The rules of Polynomial/General on an explicit algebra, for any extensionality and with no induction–recursion. The code is Counter's (`counter`), and the numbers of each parity, with the identification kept, are an algebra (`numbers`). On its generated part an element sits at the index its argument computes, and the decoding equation gives its value (`next`, `next-decodes`, `three-decodes`, `three-lands`); the value also computes at a variable extensionality, since the operation reads only the child (`three-computes`). Induction computes through its equation (`count`, `count-next`, `count-three`), uses the hypothesis at an open child (`count-is-value`), and gives the parity of every index (`parity-of-index`). The generated part is exact: an algebra of a code whose only constructor lands at ₀ can be inhabited at ₁ (`junk`, `junk-at-₁`), and its generated part has nothing there (`index-is-₀`, `nothing-at-₁`). |
| [Polynomial/Outputs](Polynomial/Outputs.agda) | Outputs polynomial in types: the output type at each index is the information of a one-sort polynomial code over `Set ℓ` (Σ, Π over small types, constants, `Set ℓ`; families of types are one). Smallness or a Mahlo reflection gives such small algebras in [Mahlo/DS/Polynomial/Outputs](../Mahlo/DS/Polynomial/Outputs.agda). |

## Univalent universes

The rules of Shulman's higher inductive–recursive univalent universe of a type-valued description, and least generations of the generated part of an algebra, from propositional resizing or from an exact classifier of propositions. IR states the rules as a record and does not inhabit them. [Mahlo/DS](../Mahlo/DS/README.md) derives these universes from the smallness principle with unique names, equivalently the ambient Mahlo rule with unique names, with elimination from a least generation; [Mahlo/DS/Univalent/Construction](../Mahlo/DS/Univalent/Construction.agda) builds a universe from an algebra with its law, unique names and a generation.

| [Univalent/Universe](Univalent/Universe.agda) | Shulman's rules for a type-valued description γ (HoTT blog, 8 June 2014), with codes at a level κ (`Universe`, a record): codes and their decoding, an introduction for every argument of γ decoding to its output, and a path constructor `ua` that the decoding sends back to its input (`Code`, `El`, `intro`, `intro-β`, `ua`, `ua-β`). Elimination into level ν (`Elimination`, a record): a method at every introduction and a path method over `ua` (`Method`, `Path-method`), with both computation rules (`elim`, `elim-intro`, `elim-ua`). Lumsdaine's observation: `ua` makes the fibers of the decoding propositions (`fibers`), hence `ua a a refl ＝ refl` (`ua-refl`). So a path method forces the motive to be a family of propositions (`forced`), every family of propositions has one (`propositional`), and elimination is induction into propositions (`induction`). |

A generation of M is one at the carrier's level κ; it is least at level ν when it lies below every closed family of propositions of level ν (`Least`). A least generation is Sattler and Vezzosi's propositional indexed W-type (LICS 2020, Def. | [Univalent/Resizing](Univalent/Resizing.agda) | Propositional resizing gives least generations. Resizing between two levels is [Foundation/Impredicative/PropositionalResizing](../Foundation/Impredicative/PropositionalResizing.agda)'s `Propositional-Resizing-Between` (a proposition of level ν with a proposition of level κ and maps both ways, `Resized`; `propositions-resize-to-their-level` at one level). The points of an algebra's carrier in every closed family of propositions of level ν (`intersection`) form a proposition under extensionality (`intersection-proposition`); resized to the carrier's level they are a generation (`resized`, `generation`), least at every level that resizes to ν (`shrunk`, `shrink`, `least`). ## Universal induction–recursion

[Universal](Universal.agda) defines one induction–recursive type for all Dybjer–Setzer descriptions, the universal type `U γ` with its decoding `value γ`, by Agda's mutual datatype and function. Decoding of introduction, native induction and root recovery hold by refl at every code. Dybjer–Setzer's induction rule follows with no extensionality, and native induction is pointwise initial with no extensionality, from the initiality of trees at the derivations of the elements. With induction on source arguments the universal type is an algebra of its description to which the initiality theorems above apply: under extensionality for functions from types at the arities' level, it is initial among the lawful algebras, and translations act on universal types with their functor laws. Its modules, and what computes and what is an identity, are in [Universal/README.md](Universal/README.md). The draft paper proof [Universal induction–recursion in simplicial sets](../--drafts--/UniversalIR.md) interprets the universal type in simplicial sets.

## Universal indexed induction–recursion

[Indexed](Indexed.agda) defines one induction–recursive family for all schemes of indexed polynomial induction–recursion, over the codes of [Polynomial](Polynomial.agda): the carriers `U s i` and their readouts `value s i`, together with the arguments of every code over them and their information. Decoding, induction and root recovery hold by refl at a variable scheme and code, with no extensionality. Native induction is pointwise initial with no extensionality; the comparison with ordinary arguments and induction on them hold under extensionality for families of information over the arities; Dybjer–Setzer's rules for general indexed codes follow with no extensionality; and Kubánek's reducibility predicate for the external Mahlo universe is a general code, also over Kripke stages and with neutral codes. Its modules are in [Indexed/README.md](Indexed/README.md). The draft paper proof [Indexed induction–recursion in simplicial sets](../--drafts--/IndexedUniversalIR.md) builds the universal family in simplicial sets.
