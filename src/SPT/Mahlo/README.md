# The Mahlo rule

The Mahlo rule for a Tarski family asks every operator on its families for a reflection: a code whose decoding carries a closure under the selected formers and the operator. At the ambient family of a universe level, whose codes are the types of that level, it is the ambient Mahlo rule, Dybjer and Setzer's external Mahlo universe (Ann. Pure Appl. Logic 2003) without elimination for its subuniverses. This folder states the rule and the decoding laws of the formers, proves the paradoxes of trees that limit which families can reflect which operators, inhabits the rule with native induction–recursion and with polynomial trees, and defines the degrees of Mahlo reflection and the strict form of the rule. Its subfolders derive induction–recursion from the ambient rule ([DS](DS/README.md)), separate the free closure from its being small and named ([Residence](Residence/README.md)), measure derivations by heights ([Heights](Heights/README.md)), and generate a Mahlo universe from a displayed seed ([Universe](Universe/README.md)). [Mahlo](../Mahlo.agda) is an index that imports every module of the folder.

## The specification

[Specification](Specification.agda) works over a Tarski family (C , D): codes C, each decoding to a type D a of level ℓ. A family of the parent is a code a with a code-valued map on D a, and an operator maps families to families. `Formers` selects parent codes: empty, unit, two, the natural numbers, products, sums and trees of families, and identity types. An interpretation `interpret : A → C` of a type A of level ℓ decodes A (`Decode`), sends its families to families of the parent (`map`), and names a code c by the points of A interpreted as c (`Name`). A closure of an operator Ψ on A (`Closure`) is an interpretation that names, in A, the selected formers of its own families and their images under Ψ:

    inner-β F : map (inner F) ＝ Ψ (map F).

A reflection of Ψ (`Reflection`) is a parent code whose decoding carries a closure, and a reflector (`Mahlo`) reflects every operator: the Mahlo rule. [Ambient](Ambient.agda) is the ambient parent of level ℓ: its codes are the types of level ℓ, each decoding to itself, `formers ℓ` selects the ordinary type formers, and `laws ℓ` gives their decoding laws, each by refl. The ambient Mahlo rule, `Mahlo (Ambient.formers ℓ)`, asks every operator on the families of `Set ℓ` for a type of level ℓ with an interpretation into `Set ℓ` that names the type formers and the operator's values at its own families.

Specification and Ambient declare these records and inhabit none of them: the rule is a hypothesis of the theorems that use it. With native induction–recursion it is inhabited, by the closures of [Relative](Relative.agda) ([DS/Realization/Discriminator](DS/Realization/Discriminator.agda)'s `reflector`).

| [Ambient](Ambient.agda) | The ambient parent of level ℓ, whose codes are the types of level ℓ decoding to themselves: its operators (`Operator ℓ`), the ordinary type formers (`formers ℓ`) and their decoding laws, each by refl (`laws ℓ`). No reflector is assumed. |
| [Setzer](Setzer.agda) | Setzer's rules for a Mahlo universe, as in Dybjer–Setzer, *Induction–recursion and initial algebras*, §6.1: every pair of operations on the universe has a subuniverse represented in it (`Operators`, `Subuniverse`, with `Coproducts` for Nₖ and +). Resident reflection gives the rules (`subuniverse`, `reflector`, `from-reflection`); under function extensionality at the branch functions they give it back (`reflection`, `to-reflection`). Pairs of operations and operators on families determine each other by refl (`split-operator`, `operator-split`), and subuniverses can be taken closed under Nₖ and + by Setzer's augmentation (`closed`, `augmented`). |
| [Terminal](Terminal.agda) | The family with one code and one point has Mahlo reflection (`reflecting`): every selected former has its unique name, every operator is reflected by the unique code, and the closure equation holds by unit η. |
| [Decoding](Decoding.agda) | The decoding laws of a parent's formers (`DecodingLaws`): the empty, unit, two-point and natural-number codes decode to `𝟘`, `𝟙`, `𝟚` and `ℕ`, lifted to the decoded level, and the product, sum, tree and identity codes to dependent function types, dependent pair types, W-types ([Foundation/Tree/W](../Foundation/Tree/W.agda)) and identity types. A closure's names give its child its own formers (`child-formers`), with laws composed from the parent's (`child-decoding`); a Mahlo reflection (`MahloReflection`) is a reflector with the parent's decoding laws, and every reflected child decodes its formers as the parent does (`reflected-decoding`). |
| [Decoding/Reindexing](Decoding/Reindexing.agda) | Reindexing a W-type's branches by a map homotopic to the identity keeps its trees up to similarity (`outward`, `inward`, `reindexed`, an `Unfolding`). |
| [Retraction](Retraction.agda) | Decoding of the selected formers up to retraction (`Retract`, `Retracts`): each decoded former, the identity of two points included, is a retract of its standard type, trees up to similarity (`Similar`, `Unfolding`), which needs no function extensionality. Retracts compose, add and act fiberwise (`_∘ʳ_`, `_∘ᵘ_`, `_⊎ʳ_`, `fiberwise`); decoding laws give them (`retracts-from-laws`), and the child of a closure inherits them (`inherited-retracts`). A binary sum is a sum code over the two-point code (`plus`, `plus-retract`), and the unit code is a retract of the unit type (`unit-retract`). |
| [Sets](Sets.agda) | The parent of small sets of level ℓ: its codes are small sets (`Small-Set`), decoded by their points. Under extensionality for the dependent functions of level ℓ the ordinary type formers keep sets, so they are its formers (`set-formers`), each decoding by refl (`set-laws`). Two small sets are identified exactly when their points are (`small-sets-are-identified-by-their-points`), and under univalence exactly when their points are equivalent (`small-set-identifications`). No reflector is assumed. |

## Trees and the limits of reflection

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Paradox](Paradox.agda) | The paradox of trees. A tree is a member of another when it is one of its subtrees, and no tree is a member of itself (`not-own-member`). So no type of a universe has that universe as a retract (`no-retraction`), and none is isomorphic to it, lifted or not (`no-isomorphism-onto-universe`, `no-lifted-isomorphism-onto-universe`). |
| [SelfNaming](SelfNaming.agda) | A family of types closed under W up to isomorphism (`Has-Trees`) has no type with its own index as a retract (`no-retract-of-the-names`), so it names no type isomorphic to its index (`no-name-of-the-names`). For the same reason no W-type is isomorphic to one of its own branchings (`no-branching-is-the-trees`). |
| [Generation](Generation.agda) | When the tree codes of a Tarski family decode exactly (`Tree-Law`), no decoding retracts onto the codes (`no-retract`), no reflection names its own carrier (`unnamed`), and a family whose every code is named in every closure of an operator (`Generated`) has no reflection of that operator (`unreflected`). Families whose head and branches are named have named former values (`formed`), with extensionality on the decoded domains only. |
| [Lifting](Lifting.agda) | Well-founded trees under lifting: a tree over A and B raises to a tree over their lifts and lowers back (`raised-tree`, `lowered-tree`), the two inverse under extensionality for the branches (`lowered-raised`, `raised-lowered`). |

## Reflected children and degrees

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Child](Child.agda) | Small spaces in the child returned by a Mahlo reflection, whose code type is literally the decoding of the returned parent code. A space of the child is a code with a code-valued map on its decoding (`Mahlo-Space`), the same datum as a family of the child (`to-family`, `from-family`, inverse by refl). The closure's inner family acts on spaces (`action`), the parent family of the action being Ψ's (`parent-family`, `action-β`), and a space decodes to a displayed space (`display`). |
| [Degree](Degree.agda) | Degrees of Mahlo reflection for a reflector with decoding laws. A child the reflector returns, given a reflector of its own, is again a Mahlo reflection (`child-reflection`). Degree 0 asks nothing, and degree n + 1 asks that every returned child carry a reflector of its own of degree n (`Degree`; `mahlo`, `child`, `next` read off its parts); a reflector of degree n + 1 has degree n (`lower-degree`). |
| [Degree/Tree](Degree/Tree.agda) | Degrees along trees: at a node each position asks every child of the reflector to be Mahlo again, with the degree of the subtree at that position, and a hole asks for nothing further (`Along`); substitution composes degrees (`Graft`, `graft`, `ungraft`). Brouwer trees are the trees of one judgment with zero, successors and limits of sequences (`Brouwer`): the finite chains present the finite degrees (`finite`, `to-finite`, `from-finite`), and the tree ω asks for every finite degree at once (`from-omega`, `to-omega`). |

## Reflectors from induction–recursion

[Relative](Relative.agda), [Universe](Universe.agda), [Universe/Boundary](Universe/Boundary.agda), [Described](Described.agda) and [Polynomial/Closure](Polynomial/Closure.agda) define induction–recursive types, and Space, Universe/Formation, Described/Discriminator and the other Polynomial modules are built on them. No other module directly in this folder or in its small subfolders uses induction–recursion.

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Relative](Relative.agda) | The induction–recursive closure of an operator Ψ over a Tarski family (C , D) with formers Q. Its codes (`Generated Q Ψ`) are the selected formers, the product, sum and tree of a family of codes, the identity type of two points, and Ψ's result and branch codes at a family of codes; `interpret` sends each code to the parent code it names, by recursion alongside the codes, as in `interpret (result a b) = pr₁ (Ψ (interpret a , interpret ∘ b))`. The codes carry a closure of Ψ (`closure`) and have dependent induction, computing at each constructor by refl (`Methods`, `induction`). This is the only induction–recursion the Mahlo rule needs. |
| [Space](Space.agda) | The Mahlo rule when the parent's codes are small displayed spaces, decoded to their totals. The returned space has Relative's codes as its base and a one-point fiber over each (`flat`), so each point of the space carries its code literally (`read`, `write`); the closure and its reflection (`closure`, `reflection`) give the rule for every choice of formers (`mahlo`). |
| [Universe](Universe.agda), [Universe/Boundary](Universe/Boundary.agda), [Universe/Formation](Universe/Formation.agda) | A small Tarski universe generated from a displayed seed U and an operator Ψ: U's base and fibers, all ordinary formers, Ψ's complete results and branches, and coded indexed boundary families of [Universe/Boundary](Universe/Boundary.agda) with their exact fills, every decoding equation judgmental (`Code`, `decode`, `Seeds`, `boundary`, `filling`). The codes form a closure of the ambient formers and Ψ (`closure`, `formers`, `laws`), hence a Mahlo reflection of the ambient family (`reflection`), and have dependent induction (`Methods`, `ind`). Formation reads the spaces of the reflected child off the universe and shows that the selected result point and complete fills, not boundaries alone, determine later formation (`no-selection-loss`, `Boundary.no-filler-loss`, `Boundary.next`). See the [Universe README](Universe/README.md). |
| [Described](Described.agda) | A small Tarski universe V that reflects every operator it can describe, inside itself and with code-level names. Descriptions (`Desc`), defined together with V, are the identity, composition, a resident family, products, sums and trees of an output, and selection over the points of a code; each reads as an operator on V's families (`operator`) and as one on families of types (`⟦_⟧`), and the two agree along the decoding (`agreement`). The code `reflected d` decodes to Relative's closure of ⟦ d ⟧ over the ambient family; once V is complete, `embed` maps that closure into V's own codes, and `coherence` relates the two decodings. So every described operator has a reflection whose carrier is a code of V (`reflect`). The definition is strictly positive because V reads its operators off codes, not off functions on its families. The hypothesis is extensionality for functions out of decoded types. |
| [Described/Discriminator](Described/Discriminator.agda) | The reflections of Described have content. Formers commute with the embedding as codes (`product-code`); a reflected action's head is the operator's actual output code (`output-code`, `output-β`); a sum after a selection returns V's sigma code over the selection's output, and a tree description V's tree code (`composed-code`, `tree-code`); a description returning one reflected child is reflected again, with that child as its output (`contained`). No carrier is derivable by V's formers and its own operator (`carrier-underivable`); each carrier is derivable by the constant operator at it, which V describes (`carrier-derivable`), and V's reflection of the constant operator at a code does not have that code as carrier (`constant-carrier`). V's codes are small, so V does not admit every small type (`unadmitting`): it reflects what it describes, but cannot absorb its level. Combined through V's two-element code, two described operators derive each one's outputs and one applied to the other's (`selected-derived`, `composed-derived`), and no carrier is derivable by its own operator and the identity together (`carrier-underivable-together`). |

## Polynomial parents

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Polynomial/Basis](Polynomial/Basis.agda) | A small parent containing the carrier of an ordinary induction–recursive universe. The small codes are Relative's closure of the identity operator over the ambient formers (`Small`, `small-decode`); as a displayed seed (`small-seed`) they generate Universe's universe at the identity operator (`Code`, `formers`, `laws`), whose code `codes` decodes to them (`codes-decode-to-small`). Under extensionality for the families and branches on decoded codes (`Realization-Extensionality`), every small code is realized by a code of the universe with the identification of their decodings, computed together (`realize`, `include`, `include-β`), so the small codes form a closure of the identity operator over the universe (`ordinary`), which the universe reflects at `codes` (`reflection`). |
| [Polynomial/Closure](Polynomial/Closure.agda) | A small induction–recursive closure of an operator Ψ whose dependent operations are literal open polynomial trees (`Code`, `interpret`). A preceding code determines each block's arity: a block is a tree of the one-color polynomial whose operations are the four kinds and whose positions are the points of that code's decoding (`Kind`, `block-polynomial`), with closure codes in its holes. A block runs to the parent code it names, each node by its kind (`run`), and result points keep their branches (`branch`). The selected formers are blocks of one node over holes (`block-form`), so the codes form a closure of Ψ, each name and the inner law by refl (`closure`). |
| [Polynomial/Generation](Polynomial/Generation.agda), [Polynomial/Generation/Formation](Polynomial/Generation/Formation.agda) | Generated small types with literal polynomial tree carriers. A diagram in a family gives a code to every index, operation and position type, its input map keeping the exact index required at each position (`Diagram`, `diagram-polynomial`). The generated codes (`Generated`, decoded by `Value`) are Basis's codes, sums and products of families, the closed trees of every diagram of generated codes at each index (`trees`, decoding by refl, `trees-β`), and repeatable materializations: the power of a code, or the fiber, total or section of a predicate into the small codes (`Capability`, `Materialized`, `power-β`, `twice-β`); functions and their higher types are products (`arrow`, `higher`, `higher-β`). Formation: tree stages over a code and higher-order functions on complete trees as later branching types (`stage-diagram`, `stage`, `leaf`, `fork`, `functional`, `again`, `higher-node`, `root`). Over the code of 𝟚, two readings of a sample function agree at the constant leaf family, yet a later dependent product is inhabited at one and empty at the other, so no family of types on the stage reads the product off the value at the constant family (`next`, `left`, `right`, `same`, `live`, `dead`, `no-loss`, `no-factor`); a complete higher-order function is an actual position in the next polynomial (`second`, `second-β`). |
| [Polynomial/Parent](Polynomial/Parent.agda), [Polynomial/Parent/Formation](Polynomial/Parent/Formation.agda) | A raised Tarski parent with small values, polynomial tree formation and repeated materialization (`Generated`, `Value`, `admit`, `Capability`, `Materialized`, `trees`), its formers decoding by refl (`formers`, `laws`). Every operator Ψ is reflected by admitting Polynomial/Closure's closure of Ψ as a code, `Value (reflected Ψ) ＝ Closure.Code (formers ℓ) Ψ` (`reflect`, `reflected`, `reflected-β`), so the parent is Mahlo unconditionally (`mahlo`), with its decoding laws (`full`). Formation, at level zero: the reflection of the identity operator is a code, with the higher type (T → T) → T of its decoding T (`reflected-identity`, `carrier-higher`, `higher-β`); one literal block with two successive operations holds a whole closure code in its holes (`assemble`, `assemble-β`); the interpreted return at the identity determines the next operator, with resident reflection whose closure satisfies the inner law by refl (`selected`, `operator`, `second`, `resident`, `resident-β`, `whole`); a later dependent sum over the chosen return is inhabited at one reading and empty at another that agrees with it at the constant family (`anchor`, `chosen`, `later`, `live`, `dead`, `no-loss`, `no-factor`); and the complete second carrier is again a materialization domain (`powers`, `higher-powers`, `twice-β`). |

## The strict rule

The strict Mahlo rule, every decoding law a strict identification in context, needs Agda's two-level type theory. It is in [TwoLevel](../TwoLevel/README.md) (`Strict-Mahlo`, its labelled step algebras, and the rule for displayed families), since this folder, like SPT's Foundation, is one-level.

## Subfolders

- [DS](DS/README.md): induction–recursion from the ambient Mahlo rule. The rule is interderivable with IR's smallness principle, and it gives Dybjer–Setzer induction–recursion with small arities and outputs in `Set ℓ` or in any type O of any level, indexed polynomial induction–recursion, the Mahlo rule of every family admitting the small types, and Shulman's univalent universes from its form with unique names. The folder also identifies the smallness principle of set-valued descriptions with the rule of the parent of small sets, and states stationary reflection, which with the rule gives every finite degree of Mahlo reflection.
- [Residence](Residence/README.md): the free closure of an operator separated from its being small and named. Residence is calibrated against Mahlo reflection, no least closure is Mahlo, and the obstructions to residence are read off the pullback square of a resident family.
- [Heights](Heights/README.md), with [Heights](Heights.agda) directly in this folder: heights of the derivations of the derivable names. They close below a small height, the least such height needs no induction–recursion, and the ambient Mahlo rule is the existence of small heights regular for the stages of every operator's codes.
- [Universe](Universe/README.md): the universe of Universe.agda generated from a displayed seed and an operator, with coded indexed boundary formation, and the spaces of its reflected child.
- [MartinLof](../MartinLof/README.md), beside this folder: a Martin-Löf type theory with Kubánek's Mahlo subuniverses whose only constants are univalence and the ambient Mahlo rule at every level, the rule stated in the theory's own syntax as `Mahlo (Ambient.formers ℓ)`; every derivation is valid in every model of the theory (`soundness`).

## Paper proofs

Drafts of paper proofs included in the release place these rules in simplicial sets, over ZFC with a strongly Mahlo cardinal:

- [Simplicial.md](../--drafts--/Simplicial.md): the ambient Mahlo rule in Kapulkin and Lumsdaine's simplicial model, alongside univalence, with its decoding laws as identities.
- [StrictMahlo.md](../--drafts--/StrictMahlo.md): the strict ambient Mahlo rule, every decoding law an equality of maps of simplicial sets, in the shape of `Strict-Mahlo` ([TwoLevel/Mahlo](../TwoLevel/Mahlo.agda)).
- [StrictDisplayMahlo.md](../--drafts--/StrictDisplayMahlo.md): the strict Mahlo rule for the universe of displays, whose operators are ambient operators with a fiber part over them.
- [UniversalIR.md](../--drafts--/UniversalIR.md) and [IndexedUniversalIR.md](../--drafts--/IndexedUniversalIR.md): the universal induction–recursive type of IR and its induction–recursive family for indexed polynomial codes ([IR](../IR/README.md)), with their realized laws as equalities of maps.
