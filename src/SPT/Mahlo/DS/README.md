# Induction–recursion from the ambient Mahlo rule

The ambient Mahlo rule is [Specification](../Specification.agda)'s `Mahlo` at the formers of [Ambient](../Ambient.agda): every operator on the families of `Set ℓ` has a small family of codes closed under the type formers and the operator. It is Dybjer and Setzer's external Mahlo universe (Ann. Pure Appl. Logic 2003) without elimination for its subuniverses. This folder derives from it, in safe Agda and without induction–recursion, every form of Dybjer–Setzer induction–recursion below, with arities and carriers in `Set ℓ` and outputs in `Set ℓ` or in any type O of any level, over the descriptions, algebras and generated parts of [IR](../../IR/README.md). It also derives from the rule the Mahlo rule of every family admitting the small types, and Shulman's univalent universes from the rule's form with unique names; it identifies the smallness principle of set-valued descriptions with the rule of the parent of small sets; and it states stationary reflection, which with the rule gives every finite degree of Mahlo reflection. Apart from [Realization/Discriminator](Realization/Discriminator.agda) and [Sorted/Realization/Discriminator](Sorted/Realization/Discriminator.agda), which define reflectors by native induction–recursion, and [Realization/Schema](Realization/Schema.agda) and [Sorted/Realization/Cedents](Sorted/Realization/Cedents.agda), which use them, no module here imports induction–recursion; none imports the standard library.

## The rule and the smallness principle

The principle, for a level ℓ ([IR/Smallness](../../IR/Smallness.agda)):

```text
Smallness ℓ  =  Π (γ : Desc (Set ℓ)). Σ (K : Set ℓ) (value : K → Set ℓ)
                  (operation : Shape γ (K , value) → K).
                  Π z. value (operation z) ＝ Mark γ (K , value) z
```

It asks for an algebra on a small carrier with its decoding law, and nothing else: no induction and no leastness. Without smallness there is always such an algebra one level up, on the outputs themselves, and its generated part is the raised fixed point μγ; the principle is equivalently a lowering of every raised fixed point, under extensionality for index families ([IR/Smallness/Lowering](../../IR/Smallness/Lowering.agda)).

## Decoding by conversion

The decoding equation is an identity. At a closed description, with a reflector whose names compute, it also holds by conversion at every constructor. With every datum and the constructor tag variable, it holds by conversion at every argument when the spine is the same under every tag. It stays an identity where the spine is not known: at a variable description, and at a variable tag whose branches have different spines.

## Outputs in any type

For any type O, the two-sorted family `Sort ℓ O = Set ℓ ⊎ O` names small types and values of O, decodes a type to itself and a value to the empty type, and has the formers on its types. For O empty it is the ambient family. The Mahlo rule for this family is interderivable with smallness for descriptions with outputs in `Sort ℓ O`, and it gives every description with outputs in O alone a small algebra. The Sorted modules repeat the ambient realization for this family; the ambient modules are kept separate, so that their closed descriptions decode by conversion.

The ambient Mahlo rule gives the two-sorted rule for every O, under extensionality for families of names, as the instance of [families admitting the small types](#families-admitting-the-small-types) at this family (`Naming.sorted`); conversely the two-sorted rule gives the ambient rule (`Naming.unsorted`), since its formers decode as the ambient ones by refl. So the ambient rule already gives small algebras of every description with outputs in any type (`Naming.outputs`), and smallness for outputs in types gives smallness for outputs in `Sort ℓ O` (`Naming.smallness`). Through Naming the decoding equations of these algebras are identities proved from extensionality; through the two-sorted induction–recursion of Sorted/Realization/Discriminator, whose names hold by refl, the operator outputs of Sorted/Discriminator's closed description decode by refl.

## Families admitting the small types

A Tarski family (C , D) with formers Q admits the small types when every small type is a retract of the decoding of a name (`Naming.Admission`); a family naming every small type does (`Naming.naming`), and so does the two-sorted family. For every such family the ambient Mahlo rule gives its Mahlo rule, under extensionality for families of names (`Naming.mahlo`).

The values the formers and an operator Φ produce are recorded by well-founded trees over a family of small types, with no induction–recursion, with a node for each former and for Φ (Naming/Trees). An applied node records its index as a choice in an earlier tree, a map from that choice's decoding into its branching type, and a choice at each branch, so a name need not be fixed by its decoding. Every code is a request over a family of the ambient closure's codes (`Value`), read as the element it selects, so every selection is named by refl. The formers and Φ at a family of codes are one node each, in the family joining the index's and the members' families with the ambient name of the index's decoding, and the node's input is the given family along extensionality (`node-β`). The ambient rule, applied to the operator returning all trees with a selected element, gives the small types needed, and the carrier is moved to an admitted name along its retraction (`transfer`).

Conversely, for a family naming every small type whose formers decode as the ambient ones (`DecodingLaws`), its Mahlo rule gives the ambient rule: an ambient operator acts on the names of its types (`lifted`), and a closure under it decodes to an ambient closure (`typed`, `ambient`). So for such a family the two rules are equivalent, under extensionality; the two-sorted family is one (`sorted-laws`, `unsorted`).

## Cedents and derivations

A cedent of a family (C , D) is a predicate on its names. A cedent is closed under the formers and an operator Φ when it contains the constant formers' names and, at a family of its names, the family formers' names and Φ's index and members (`Closed`). The names derivable from a cedent Γ form an ordinary inductive family over the names, with no induction–recursion (`Derivable`): the least closed cedent containing Γ (`least`). So derivability is a closure operator on cedents (`seeded`, `monotone`, `idempotent`). The names of any closure of the operator form a closed cedent, under extensionality for families of names (`named-closed`), so every closure names every name derivable from nothing (`derivable-named`).

The closure built from the ambient rule (Naming) names exactly the derivable names: every selection of a tree is derivable, by induction on its stage, so the closure lies in every closed cedent with no extensionality, and with `derivable-named` the two cedents agree. Its trees are a small type of derivations of the derivable names. The two-sorted induction–recursive closure names the same names, so induction–recursion and the ambient Mahlo rule give two small types of derivations of one inductive family, each naming what the other names. Their heights are compared in [Heights](../Heights/README.md).

## Univalent universes

Shulman's univalent universe of a description (HoTT blog, 8 June 2014) is the induction–recursive universe with a path constructor `ua` from identifications of decodings to identifications of codes, which the decoding sends back to its input. Read through fibers, as Sattler and Vezzosi do (LICS 2020), its free part is a family of propositions over the decodings, inductively generated: a propositional indexed W-type, a higher inductive family. Lumsdaine's observation, in Shulman's post, is that this one path constructor makes the fibers of the decoding propositions. Here the universe is derived in book type theory, with no higher inductive type, for every description with outputs in `Set ℓ`, with small codes, from two inputs:

- **The smallness principle with unique names** (Univalent/Smallness's `Univalent-Smallness`): every description has a small algebra with its law whose decoding is an embedding, each `Name (value M) X` a proposition. At each description such an algebra is Shulman's universe without elimination on its own carrier, the path rule being the names of identified decodings, and a universe's introductions are such an algebra by Lumsdaine's lemma (IR/Univalent/Universe's `fibers`). Since Ambient's translations keep the decoding literally, it is also **the ambient Mahlo rule with unique names** (Univalent/Reflector's `Reflector`: in every closure, interpretation is an embedding), with the extensionality of the plain equivalence.
- **A least generation** of that algebra ([IR/Univalent/Generation](../../IR/Univalent/Generation.agda)): a closed family of propositions on its carrier at its level, below every closed family of propositions of the levels eliminated into. Propositional resizing gives one ([IR/Univalent/Resizing](../../IR/Univalent/Resizing.agda)), by resizing the intersection of all closed families from ℓ + 1 to ℓ. An exact classifier of propositions at ℓ gives one with no resizing ([IR/Univalent/Classifier](../../IR/Univalent/Classifier.agda)), least at ℓ and so eliminating into level ℓ. Excluded middle at ℓ gives such a classifier, where the resizing route asks for excluded middle at ℓ + 1.

Both inputs are needed, for different reasons. Without unique names, closing the image of the closure under Π needs choice over the arity, a code for each member of the family; with unique names, a family of propositions gives the family of names. Without a least generation, the generated part would be a truncated inductive family, and closing that under Π needs choice for the same reason.

The eliminator's path method, whose endpoints are variables, forces the motive to be a family of propositions (IR/Univalent/Universe's `forced`), so elimination is exactly induction into propositions (`propositional`, `induction`): type-directed definitions go through contractible motives, as in Shulman's post. Every computation rule then holds as an identity, none by refl; the decoding laws are identities too.

Whether the plain rule gives its form with unique names is `Names-Passage` (Univalent/Smallness), declared and not inhabited: the passage from small lawful algebras to small lawful algebras whose decodings are embeddings. A closure with unique names collects names over the arity itself (Univalent/Completion), and collecting names is weakly initial covers relative to the closure (Univalent/Covering). Under univalence and extensionality, with no excluded middle, choice or resizing, the passage holds given completions into the small types and weakly initial covers of every small type, with no set asked for (Univalent/Types' `type-names-passage`), and given completions and covered Strong Collection in the closures of the reflector from smallness (`collection-passage`). With least generations at ℓ, the covers need only exist merely, under one truncation for all small types (Univalent/Smallness/Modal's `mere-covers-passage`). It also holds given completions and heights regular for relations, or fans of completed codes that bound merely named families ([Heights](../Heights/README.md)). Naming the members of a family over a named cover needs no descent datum: each member is the limit of the maps out of the iterated joins of its fiber ([Foundation/Joins](../../Foundation/Joins.agda), Univalent/Gluing), so no coherence above identity types enters at any truncation level.

## Set-valued descriptions

A set-valued description has small sets ([Sets](../Sets.agda)) as indices and outputs, `Desc (Small-Set ℓ)`, and set arities when the arity of every σ and δ node is a set (`Set-Arities`). Its smallness principle (Sets/Smallness's `Set-Smallness`) is the rule of the parent of small sets, closures on small types under every operator, in both directions, and so is its form with unique names (`Univalent-Set-Smallness`). A set-valued description reads as one with outputs in types, its outputs by their points and each δ followed by a σ over the sethood of the inputs, which at a family of small sets is that family's own proofs, so shapes read literally by the η-law of small sets. A closure of the parent of sets under the lift of an ambient operator that keeps families of sets is an ambient closure on the same carrier, since the parent's formers decode to the ambient ones by refl; the frontier operator of a reading keeps families of sets, so Realization applies, and its algebra reads back on the closure's own carrier. With Univalent/Covering, the passage from the principle to its form with unique names holds given completions into the small sets and global weakly initial covers by sets, with no excluded middle and no choice.

## Stationary reflection

Smallness is the Mahlo rule of the ambient family: every operator has a closure on a small carrier. Degree one asks every such closure to be Mahlo itself, a small universe reflecting all of its own operators: an internal Mahlo set inside the universe. The universe above gives Mahlo-ness only to the full universe of a level (Setzer's internal V read as `Set ℓ` inside `Set (ℓ+1)`; see [Residence](../Residence/README.md)), and a reflector built from smallness has least closures, which are not Mahlo ([Residence/Unreflected](../Residence/Unreflected.agda)). The principle that gives degree one is stationary reflection. A predicate S on universes is stationary when every operator has a reflection whose universe satisfies S; the rule at S gives every operator a reflection in whose child S is stationary. At the unit predicate the rule's conclusion is degree one, and the rule at each degree gives the next.

A universe is a reflection of the identity operator. A universe of its child is read in the parent by Residence's resident composition at the identity ([Residence/Subclosure](../Residence/Subclosure.agda)'s `onward` and `through`, [Residence/Reflection](../Residence/Reflection.agda)'s `compose`), and its resident families compose as Residence/Families' `compose`, by refl (Residence/Reflection's `resident-compose`). So the grandchildren the rule reads are Residence's composites, with the grandchild's own former codes kept literally.

The rule is a principle, as the Mahlo rule is: nothing here inhabits it. `not-least` and Stationary/Derivable cover every parent whose formers decode up to retraction ([Retraction](../Retraction.agda)'s `Retracts`); `retracts-from-laws` at Ambient's `laws` gives the retractions of the ambient formers, so the refutations apply at the ambient family.

## Models, on paper

Drafts of paper proofs in this release place these rules in simplicial sets, over ZFC with a strongly Mahlo cardinal, and inaccessible cardinals above it for the levels above.

- [Simplicial.md](../../--drafts--/Simplicial.md), the ambient Mahlo rule in simplicial sets: `Mahlo (Ambient.formers ℓ)` has an element in Kapulkin and Lumsdaine's simplicial model, alongside univalence, with its decoding laws as identities. The derivations here of induction–recursion with outputs in `Set ℓ` or in any type, of indexed polynomial induction–recursion, and of the Mahlo rule of every family admitting the small types use extensionality only as explicit hypotheses for named function types, all of which hold under univalence, so by this draft they would hold in that model, with decoding as an identity. There the closure of an operator is a smaller universe interpreted by its inclusion, an embedding, so the rule would hold with unique names. Excluded middle holds in the simplicial model (Kapulkin and Lumsdaine, TAC 2020), and at a single level it gives an exact classifier, which suffices for elimination into the level of the codes (Univalent/Reflector's `decided-elimination`); so Shulman's univalent universes would hold there too.
- [StrictMahlo.md](../../--drafts--/StrictMahlo.md), the strict ambient Mahlo rule in simplicial sets: every decoding law an equality of maps. It gives no unique names.
- [UniversalIR.md](../../--drafts--/UniversalIR.md) and [IndexedUniversalIR.md](../../--drafts--/IndexedUniversalIR.md): IR's universal induction–recursive type and its induction–recursive family for indexed polynomial codes in simplicial sets, with their realized laws as equalities of maps. Strict decoding at a variable description comes from there, not from the realization here: in two-level Agda inner data cannot be eliminated into outer statements, and the realization transports along the reflector's laws.

## Not here

- A derivation of the rule with unique names from the plain rule with no further hypothesis, that is, an inhabitant of `Names-Passage`; its conditional forms are above. Whether the plain rule gives the collection without weakly initial covers is open, equivalently whether the completion of some closure of each operator names its sums (Univalent/Sums); it suffices that the fans of the reflections of completed codes bound merely named families ([Heights](../Heights/README.md)), and completions follow from higher inductive types (Rijke's join construction).
- The category of algebras as such: identities and composites exist, with the unit and associativity laws on valuations ([IR/Morphism/Composition](../../IR/Morphism/Composition.agda)), but the laws are not compared as morphisms.
- Morphisms of indexed polynomial algebras ([IR/Polynomial/Algebra](../../IR/Polynomial/Algebra.agda)) and their initiality.
- Least generations as a higher inductive family (Sattler and Vezzosi's propositional indexed W-type). Universes closed under such families are not known in simplicial sets (Lumsdaine and Shulman, §1; Shulman 2019, §1), so the model route goes through resizing.
- General higher induction–recursion: path constructors in arbitrary descriptions, with decoding computing on them. Only the univalent universe of a description is derived.
- The converse for a family that admits the small types only up to a retraction: `Naming.ambient` needs each small type to be the decoding of a name, so that an ambient operator acts on names.
- Dybjer–Setzer descriptions with polynomial outputs through the embedding of Dybjer–Setzer codes into PN (Ghani, McBride, Nordvall Forsberg and Spahn, Prop. 28).
- A degree carried along admission. The reflector `Naming.mahlo` builds has no degree one (Stationary/Derivable's `naming-degree-zero`), since its universes name only derivable names. A transfer would need children with names outside the derivable ones, and ambient children that name the request types of their own operators: a principle stronger than degree one, not formulated here.
