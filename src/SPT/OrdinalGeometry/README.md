# Ordinal Geometry

The modules of Ordinal Geometry on SPT's own base that [Mahlo/Heights](../Mahlo/Heights.agda) and the modules under it use: presentations of countable ordinals with their maps, sums and successors, closed heights with the ordinal of ordinals as a height, the ranks of presentations, and the boundaries of reflections. With them is [Height/Hartogs](Height/Hartogs.agda), the Hartogs boundary that [Mahlo/Heights/Hartogs](../Mahlo/Heights/Hartogs.agda) compares with its own Hartogs height. A point x of a presentation is read as its predecessor domain ∂x = Σ y. y < x. Every module is `--safe --without-K` and is written on SPT's base, the identity type and builtin types of [Base](../Base.agda) with SPT's Foundation and Mahlo modules; none uses the cubical library or the standard library.

Part I, Ordinal Geometry, is released in [Cubical/OrdinalGeometry](../Cubical/OrdinalGeometry), built on the standard and cubical libraries, and described on the [front page](../../../README.md). Eleven of the modules here have a module of the same name there, with the same constructions, some results being on one side only; Height/Hartogs, Height/Presentation, Height/Relabeling and Regularity are only here.

## Presentations

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Presentation/CountableOrdinal](Presentation/CountableOrdinal.agda) | The record `Countable-Ordinal𝒫` of presentations of countable ordinals, independent of any notation system: a well-founded strict linear order modulo an equivalence, both type-valued, with a comparison and a partial ℕ-enumeration covering every point up to equivalence, which returns the exact representative used at a successful index. The laws of one presentation (`Presentation`); bounded strict embeddings `_≺𝒫_`, well-founded (`Embedding.wf`); the countable diagonal of an ℕ-family, strictly above every member (`Diagonal.𝒫`); equivalences of presentations `_≃𝒫_`. No ℕ-family covers all presentations, up to identification (`Exact`), up to equivalence (`Shadow`) or cofinally (`Cofinality`, through `presentations-bound-families`). The ω₁ boundary, one point above every presentation (`Boundary`), and the record `𝒫ω₁𝒢` of ω₁ geometries over it, inhabited by `PresentedOmegaOne.Geometry.canonical`. |
| [Regularity](Regularity.agda) | The ω₁ pattern for any class of ordinals with a strict and a weak relation, the strict one irreflexive and a strict step followed by a weak one strict: if every family indexed by a type of an index class has a strict upper bound (`Bounded-Families`), no such family covers the class in the weak relation (`no-family-is-cofinal`). It is stated for three classes: the heights of a universe with the families indexed by its types (Height/Ordinals, `heights-bound-families`), the child trees of a reflection with the families indexed by the points of its codes (Height/Reflection, `child-trees-bound-families`), and the countable presentations with their ℕ-families (Presentation/CountableOrdinal, `presentations-bound-families`). Presentations keep decidable orders and their equivalence ≈, heights only ∼ and least upper bounds of small families; they are compared by rank (Height/Presentation). |
| [Presentation/CountablyPresented](Presentation/CountablyPresented.agda), [Presentation/CountablyPresented/LowerCone](Presentation/CountablyPresented/LowerCone.agda) | The record `Geometry` of countably presented geometries: comparison and order are global, every bound x keeps one literal partial ℕ-enumeration `enum↓ x` of exactly its strict lower cone (`enum↓-sound`), and trichotomy is asked only of pairs below one bound; the record asks for no global trichotomy, antisymmetry, quotient, uniqueness of indices or representatives, least upper bounds or notation. Every presentation gives a geometry at its level, by filtering its enumeration below the bound (`from𝒫`, `From𝒫.filter`). The strict lower cone Σ x. x < b of a point as a countable presentation (`𝒫`), its strict witness literal and ignored by both relations; it does not assert that independently given points are comparable. |
| [Presentation/Enumeration](Presentation/Enumeration.agda) | The record `Exact-Enumeration` of exact enumerations: a partial ℕ-sequence listing every point by literal identification, with no quotient or equivalence of presentations. An entry of a partial sequence is present only at `just` (`Entry`), so an occurrence keeps its index and selected value with no stored identification (`Occurrence`), and the occurrences of a sequence are themselves exactly enumerated (`occurrence-enumeration`). |
| [Presentation/Successor](Presentation/Successor.agda) | The successor P + 1 (`presentation-successor`): the points of P with one new top above them all, with the order of P and its enumeration shifted by one. It is the diagonal successor block of Presentation/CountableOrdinal at the constant family of P (`constant-presentation-family`). No quotient or choice is used. |
| [Presentation/Principal](Presentation/Principal.agda) | The predecessors Σ y. y < x of a point as a presentation (`For.lower`), included below x (`For.inclusion`) and read inside the predecessors of a larger point (`For.Inside`: `restriction`, `strict`). The predecessors of the new top of P + 1 are P (`Successor.Top`), and those of an old point are its old predecessors (`Successor.Old`). These inverse laws hold on the carriers and use no uniqueness of the witnesses of P. |
| [Presentation/Sum](Presentation/Sum.agda) | The ordered sum P + Q (`_+_`): every point of P precedes every point of Q; P enters by an initial map (`left`) and Q by an order embedding (`right`), and below a point y of Q the predecessor domain in P + Q receives P followed by ∂y (`below`). Predecessor domains ∂x and their maps (narrowing to a smaller point, nesting inside a larger one, the cone of a segment's ceiling); the successor P + 1 with its top, the embeddings of P and of anything embedded in P into it (`onward`, `to-next`), and the extension of a cone map to it (`cap`), below every point above its top (`cap-below`). |
| [Presentation/Map](Presentation/Map.agda) | The records `Embedding`, `Initial` and `Segment`. An order embedding reflects < and ≈ (`embedding-reflect`, `embedding-reflect-≈`), never descends (`nondecreasing`) and admits no bounded embedding back (`no-reverse`). An initial map keeps a preimage for every predecessor of an image point; initial maps compose, are pointwise unique up to ≈ (`Uniqueness.pointwise`), give an equivalence when opposite (`antisymmetry`), and are onto when there is an embedding back (`surjective`, `isomorphism`). A principal segment covers its domain below its ceiling and so extends to the successor (`segment-closed`). |

## Heights

A closed height is a closed tree compared by the simulation order; a height of level ℓ is a closed tree whose labels are the types of level ℓ, each node branching over its label.

| Module | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Height/Simulation](Height/Simulation.agda) | Closed trees and their relations, for any labels and branchings: structural equality ≈ (equal labels and related children), ancestry ◃ and < (immediate and proper descendants), and the simulation order ⊑ and ⊏ with its equivalence ∼. Ancestry describes how a tree is built; the simulation order compares trees by their branching alone (`below`, `under`). Both strict relations are well-founded, ancestry lies inside the strict simulation order (`<→⊏`), and structural equality gives equivalence (`≈→∼`); the shape equivalence ≅ retains an isomorphism of branch types at every node, with `≈→≅` and `≅→∼`. The heights of a universe and the boundaries of reflections are compared in this order. |
| [Height/Ordinals](Height/Ordinals.agda) | The ordinal of ordinals as a height, the ω₁ geometry one level up with heights in place of presentations. Ord(ℓ) is the height of level ℓ + 1 whose children are exactly the heights of level ℓ, lifted: every height lies below it as a child (`upper`), everything below it lies below a height (`bounded`), and it is least (`least`). Every small family of heights has its fan as an exact strict upper bound (`fan`) and its join as a least upper bound (`join`, `join-upper`, `join-least`). A child of a height is its subtree at a position of the root, and a height strictly below it lies below one (`child-position`, `strict-position`, `below-child-is-strict`); descendants are subtrees at routes (`descendant-position`, `descent-route`, `node-height`); a family below the children of a fan with a member strictly above each child has its join equivalent to the fan (`cofinal-join-is-fan`). The heights bound their small families (`heights-bound-families`), so no small family is cofinal (`not-cofinal`), no small type is isomorphic to the heights (`heights-are-not-small`), and Ord(ℓ) lies below no height of level ℓ (`not-small`): the Burali-Forti argument for heights. Every small well-founded relation has a canonical height, strictly monotone (`rank`), whose whole lies below Ord(ℓ); a closed tree over any labels whose branchings are types of level ℓ is read as a height of level ℓ, its order kept (`decoded`). |
| [Height/Relabeling](Height/Relabeling.agda) | Heights keep their shape under relabeling. A label map with the positions of each image read back as those of its label is a relabeling of branching signatures ([Foundation/Signature/Relabeling](../Foundation/Signature/Relabeling.agda); `branching-relabeling`), whose tree action (`relabel-tree` of [Foundation/Tree/Relabeling](../Foundation/Tree/Relabeling.agda)) sends closed trees to closed trees with the same branching (`relabel`); when the read-back has a section, the simulation order is kept (`relabel-⊑`, `relabel-⊏`, `relabel-∼`). Height/Ordinals reads its lift of a height one level up and its reading of a tree over decoded labels as relabelings (`lift-height`, `decoded`). |
| [Height/Presentation](Height/Presentation.agda) | The heights of presented ordinals: the ranks of Height/Ordinals at a presentation's own order and well-foundedness. The rank of P has the ranks of its points as children (`whole-spectrum`, `whole-child`, `rank-at-spectrum`); equivalent points have ranks below each other and smaller points smaller ranks (`rank-at-equivalent`, `rank-at-strict`), and ∂x has the rank of x (`nested`, `von-neumann`). Monotone maps raise ranks (`rank-at-map`, `whole-map`), P ≺𝒫 Q puts rank P below the rank of its ceiling (`whole-ceiling`, `whole-bound`), and extensionality gives `rank-at-unique`. |
| [Height/Reflection](Height/Reflection.agda) | Heights of an arbitrary reflection, in the geometry of its parent: a Tarski family (C , D) with formers, in which a reflection of an operator Ψ returns a parent code r with a closure on D r, the child ([Mahlo/Specification](../Mahlo/Specification.agda)). The parent's tree codes are assumed to unfold onto their trees up to similarity (the record `Unfolds`, a hypothesis on the parent, given by exact decoding laws, `lawful`, and by decoding up to retraction, `retracted`). The boundary of a parent family P is the node at the tree code of P whose positions are the P-trees; the boundary of a reflection is that of the family of all child codes, Ω_R = node (tree (r , interpret)) (λ q → embed (unfold q)), so its positions are the child trees. Ω lies above every child tree, is regular for families indexed by points of child codes (`child-trees-bound-families`), is closed under Ψ, and lies above every tree whose labels are named in the child. So Ω is excluded from its child, as ε₀ is from the notations below it: no child tree reaches it (`excluded`) and it is no named tree (`boundary-unnamed`); the child names neither the tree code of its own family nor its carrier (`tree-unnamed`, `carrier-unnamed`), and Ψ never returns the child's own codes (`codes-unreturned`): closure under an operator is a condition, failing for the constant operator returning them, at the family of the unit code (`unit-family`, `not-closed`). A name of a parent code in the child names it (`name-naming`). With the formers decoded up to retraction, the child's empty, unit and two codes and every code naming a parent code build child trees above their parts (`leaf`, `successor`, `fork`, `spread`), and the class of a child code has its boundary below Ω (`child-class`, `child-class-below`). No particular parent or reflector is assumed, so the statements hold inside every reflected child that carries a reflector. |
| [Height/Hartogs](Height/Hartogs.agda) | The Hartogs boundary of every code, for a parent with a classifier ([Foundation/Impredicative/Classification](../Foundation/Impredicative/Classification.agda)) and its formers decoded up to retraction. The classifier turns the subsets of a decoding D a into a decoding (`subsets`), a subset branching over the code of its members (`members`); the trees of this family are the subset trees of a, and their boundary is the Hartogs boundary H(a), a parent tree (`hartogs`). Every well-founded code-valued relation on D a has strictly monotone ranks among the subset trees (`rank-strict`), so its height lies strictly below H(a) (`founded-below`), and H(a) is the height of no such relation (`not-height`). Conversely, code-level subsets of every decoding, retaining every code-valued predicate, give a classifier (`classifying`): the positions of the Hartogs boundaries are code-level exactly when the parent has a classifier. The ℕ-branching trees embed as subset trees of ℕ, Ω_ℕ ⊑ H(ℕ) (`naturals-below`). H(a) is not shown to be least among bounds of the heights; it is no stage or boundary of an analysis from below, since subset trees have no enumeration and no decidable comparison, and the boundary of the presentations is the presented ω₁ of Presentation/CountableOrdinal. |
